Exponents and Exponential Growth

Adding the same amount again and again gives a straight line. Multiplying by the same amount again and again gives something that starts slowly and then explodes, or shrinks towards zero without ever reaching it.

  • Use the exponent rules (product, quotient, power of a power) by counting factors.
  • Explain why b⁰ = 1 and b−n = 1/bn, and write numbers in scientific notation.

1Multiplying by the same number, again and again

A bacterium splits into 2. Each of those splits again: 4. Then 8, 16, 32…. After 5 splits there are 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32 cells. Writing out all the 2s gets tiring, so we write 2⁵: the base 2 is the number being multiplied, and the exponent 5 says how many times. The whole thing, 2⁵ = 32, is called a power of 2. Read it as “2 to the 5th”. Two powers have their own names: b² is “b squared” (the area of a square of side b), and b³ is “b cubed” (the volume of a cube).

Careful: 2⁵ is not 2 × 5 = 10. The exponent counts how many 2s are multiplied together, and that grows far faster. Start with a things and multiply by b at every step: after n steps you have a·bn. The number b is the growth factor (if it’s bigger than 1) or decay factor (if it’s between 0 and 1).

2The exponent rules are just counting

You never need to memorise the exponent rules if you remember what an exponent counts. Write x³ as x·x·x and x² as x·x:

  • Product rule. x³·x² = (x·x·x)·(x·x): five x’s in a row, so x⁵. Multiplying powers of the same base adds the exponents.
  • Quotient rule. x⁵/x² = (x·x·x·x·x)/(x·x): two x’s cancel top and bottom, leaving x³. Dividing subtracts the exponents.
  • Power rule. (x³)² = x³·x³: two groups of three x’s, six in all, so x⁶. A power of a power multiplies the exponents.

Each rule works only when the bases are the same: x³·y² has nothing to combine. And (xy)³ = x³y³, because three copies of xy contain three x’s and three y’s.

3Zero and negative exponents

What could 2⁰ or 2−3 mean? You can’t multiply zero 2s, or minus three 2s. Instead, follow the pattern: 2³ = 8, 2² = 4, 2¹ = 2. Each step to the left divides by 2. Keep going: 2⁰ = 1, 2−1 = ½, 2−2 = ¼, 2−3 = ⅛. So

\[ b^0 = 1, \qquad b^{-n} = \frac{1}{b^n} \qquad (b \ne 0). \]

The quotient rule agrees: x³/x³ = 1, and also x3−3 = x⁰. And x²/x⁵ leaves three x’s on the bottom: 1/x³ = x−3. A negative exponent means “this many factors in the denominator”, so 1/bn is the reciprocal of bn. It does not make the number negative: 2−3 = ⅛ is positive.

Powers of 10 give scientific notation, a short way to write very big and very small numbers: a number from 1 up to (not including) 10, times a power of 10. The distance to the Sun, 150,000,000 km, is 1.5 × 10⁸ km; a bacterium 0.000002 m long is 2 × 10−6 m. The exponent counts how many places the decimal point moves.

4Adding versus multiplying

A linear pattern adds the same amount each step: 5, 8, 11, 14… (+3 each time). Its graph is a straight line (3.B.5). An exponential pattern multiplies by the same amount each step: 5, 10, 20, 40… (×2 each time). To tell them apart from a table, look at the differences (next − previous) and the ratios (next ÷ previous). Constant differences mean linear; constant ratios mean exponential.

Exponential growth always wins in the end. Even 1.01n (growing 1% a step) eventually passes any straight line, however steep, because each step adds 1% of an ever-bigger amount. Doubling shows this most dramatically. Fold a 0.1 mm sheet of paper in half 42 times and, if you could, it would be thick enough to reach the Moon. An old story says a king offered a reward of rice: 1 grain on the first square of a chessboard, 2 on the second, 4 on the third, doubling each time. The 64th square alone needs 2⁶³ grains, more rice than the world grows in centuries.

5Growth and decay models

An exponential function has the form y = a·bx, where a is the initial value (at x = 0) and b is the factor per step. Percent changes turn into factors:

  • Growing by r% each step multiplies by b = 1 + r/100. A video whose views rise 50% a day is multiplied by 1.5 each day.
  • Shrinking by r% each step multiplies by b = 1 − r/100. Losing 20% each step means keeping 80%: b = 0.8.

The time it takes to double is the doubling time; for decay, the time to halve is the half-life. They stay the same however much you start with: bacteria that double every 20 minutes go 100 → 200 → 400, and also 1,000,000 → 2,000,000, in each 20 minutes. Then y = a·2t/D for doubling time D, and y = a·(½)t/H for half-life H. Carbon-14 has a half-life of about 5,730 years, which is how archaeologists date old bones and wood. A hot cup of tea cools the same way, except it is the gap between the tea and the room that halves, so the tea approaches room temperature, not zero.

Not everything that shrinks is exponential. A phone battery in steady use drops by about the same number of percentage points each hour: that’s linear, and it really does hit zero.

6Traps and non-examples

Compare

Easy to get wrong

✕ 2⁵ = 2 × 5

2 × 5 adds five 2s: 10. 2⁵ multiplies five 2s: 32. The gap explodes: 2 × 20 = 40, but 2²⁰ is over a million.

✕ x³ · x⁴ = x¹²

Count the tiles: three x’s next to four x’s make seven, so x⁷. Multiplying exponents is the rule for a power of a power, (x³)⁴ = x¹².

✕ −50% then +50% gets you back

100 × 0.5 = 50, then 50 × 1.5 = 75. Percent changes multiply: 0.5 × 1.5 = 0.75, a 25% loss overall. Also watch −3² = −9 but (−3)² = 9: the exponent applies only to what it touches.

7Now, in symbols

\[ b^n = \underbrace{b \cdot b \cdots b}_{n \text{ factors}}, \qquad b^m b^n = b^{m+n}, \qquad \frac{b^m}{b^n} = b^{m-n}, \qquad (b^m)^n = b^{mn}, \qquad (ab)^n = a^n b^n \]
\[ b^0 = 1, \qquad b^{-n} = \frac{1}{b^n}, \qquad \text{scientific notation: } c \times 10^k,\ 1 \le c < 10 \]
\[ y = a\,b^{x}, \qquad b = 1 + \frac{r}{100} \text{ (growth)},\quad b = 1 - \frac{r}{100} \text{ (decay)}, \qquad y = a\cdot 2^{t/D}, \quad y = a\cdot\left(\tfrac12\right)^{t/H} \]

Python works with whole numbers of any size exactly, which makes the chessboard easy to check:

total = 0                                   # running total of grains
for square in range(1, 65):                 # squares 1 to 64
    total += 2 ** (square - 1)              # square n holds 2^(n-1) grains
print(total)                                # 18446744073709551615
print(total == 2 ** 64 - 1)                 # True: the total is one less than the next power of 2

folds = 0                                   # how many folds so far
thickness = 0.1                             # millimetres
while thickness < 384_400 * 1_000_000:      # Earth–Moon distance in millimetres
    thickness *= 2                          # each fold doubles the thickness
    folds += 1                              # count the fold
print(folds)                                # 42

9Words to know

PowerA number written as bn, such as 2⁵ = 32.
BaseThe number being multiplied, b.
ExponentHow many times the base is multiplied, n.
Squared / cubedb² and b³: the area of a square and the volume of a cube with side b.
Exponent rulesProduct: add exponents. Quotient: subtract. Power of a power: multiply.
Zero exponentb⁰ = 1 for any b ≠ 0.
Negative exponentb−n = 1/bn: factors in the denominator, not a negative number.
Reciprocal1 divided by a number; the reciprocal of 8 is ⅛.
Scientific notationc × 10k with 1 ≤ c < 10, such as 1.5 × 10⁸.
Linear changeAdding the same amount each step: constant differences, a straight-line graph.
Exponential changeMultiplying by the same factor each step: constant ratios.
Exponential functiony = a·bx.
Initial valuea, the amount at the start (x = 0).
Growth factorb > 1: the multiplier each step. Growing r% means b = 1 + r/100.
Decay factor0 < b < 1. Shrinking r% means b = 1 − r/100.
Exponential growth / decayRepeated multiplication by a factor above 1 / between 0 and 1.
Doubling timeHow long exponential growth takes to double; the same at any size.
Half-lifeHow long exponential decay takes to halve; about 5,730 years for carbon-14.
