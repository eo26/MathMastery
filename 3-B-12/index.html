Chapter 3 · Algebra

Transforming Graphs

Learn a handful of basic graphs well, and you can sketch thousands more: every y = a·f(b(x − h)) + k is the graph of f slid, stretched and flipped. Changes outside the brackets move the graph up and down the way you’d expect; changes inside move it sideways, and backwards.

Basics you are here Undergraduate Masters

On this page

  • Shift a graph up, down, left and right: f(x) + k and f(x − h).
  • Stretch, compress and reflect it: a·f(x), f(bx), −f(x) and f(−x).
  • Combine moves into y = a·f(b(x − h)) + k, track where any point goes, and see why the order of moves matters.
  • Recognise even and odd functions by their symmetry.

1Outside moves: up, down, taller, flipped

Start with a parent function, a basic graph you know well, such as y = x². Changes made after f has done its work act on the outputs, the y-values, and do just what they say.

  • y = f(x) + 3 adds 3 to every height: the graph moves up 3. Subtracting moves it down. That’s a vertical shift.
  • y = 2f(x) doubles every height: a vertical stretch by 2, which pulls the graph away from the x-axis. ½f(x) halves the heights, a vertical compression. Points on the x-axis stay put.
  • y = −f(x) makes every height its opposite: a reflection in the x-axis.

2Inside moves: sideways, and backwards

Changes made before f acts change the input, and they work the opposite way to how they look.

  • y = f(x − 3) moves the graph right 3, not left. Why: the new graph at x = 3 shows what the old one showed at 0. Everything happens 3 later. That’s a horizontal shift.
  • y = f(2x) squeezes the graph towards the y-axis by a factor of 2: the new graph reaches at x = 1 what the old one reached at x = 2. A horizontal compression; f(½x) is a horizontal stretch.
  • y = f(−x) is a reflection in the y-axis: left and right swap.

Put together: y = a·f(b(x − h)) + k sends each point (x, y) of the parent to

\[ (x,\ y) \;\longmapsto\; \Big(\frac{x}{b} + h,\ \ a\,y + k\Big). \]

    3Order matters

    Moves don’t always commute. Stretch y = x² by 2 and then lift it 3: y = 2x² + 3, lowest point (0, 3). Lift it 3 first and then stretch by 2: the lift gets stretched too, y = 2(x² + 3) = 2x² + 6. Same two moves, different graphs.

    The form a·f(b(x − h)) + k is built so the safe order is: horizontal stretch or flip (b), then vertical stretch or flip (a), then the shifts (h and k). Be careful with forms like f(2x − 6): factor out the 2 first, f(2(x − 3)), so the shift is 3, not 6. Shifts in different directions, or a stretch and a shift in different directions, can be done in any order.

    4Symmetry: even and odd functions

    Some graphs don’t change under a reflection. If f(−x) = f(x) for every x, the graph is its own mirror image in the y-axis, like x², |x| and x⁴ − 3x². Such a function is called even. If f(−x) = −f(x), reflecting in both axes (a half-turn about the origin) gives the graph back, like x³ and 1/x. That’s an odd function. The names come from powers: x to an even power is even, to an odd power is odd. Most functions, like x² + x or 2x, are neither.

    Explore

    Mirror test and half-turn test

    The purple curve is f. Blue dashed is its mirror image in the y-axis, f(−x); orange dashed is its half-turn about the origin, −f(−x). If one of them lands exactly on f, the function is even (blue) or odd (orange).

    5Transformations at work

    Changing units, delaying a start, turning up the volume or dragging a picture across a screen are all transformations of a graph. Recognising them saves recomputing anything: the shape is already known.

    6Traps and non-examples

    Compare

    Easy to get wrong

    ✕ f(x + 2) moves right

    At x = −2 the new graph shows what the old one showed at 0, so everything happens 2 earlier: it moves left. Inside the bracket, signs work backwards.

    ✕ f(2x − 6) is a shift of 6

    Factor first: f(2x − 6) = f(2(x − 3)). Squeeze by 2, then shift right 3. The vertex of (2x − 6)² is at x = 3, where the bracket is 0.

    ✕ “odd” means not even

    x² + x is neither: its mirror image (blue) and half-turn (orange) both miss. Most functions are neither even nor odd; only 0 is both.

    9Words to know

    Parent functionA basic graph used as a starting point: x², √x, |x|, 1/x, x³, 2x.
    TransformationA change that moves, stretches or flips a graph.
    Vertical shiftf(x) + k: up k (down if k < 0).
    Horizontal shiftf(x − h): right h (left if h < 0).
    Vertical stretch / compressiona·f(x): heights times a; a stretch if |a| > 1, a compression if |a| < 1.
    Horizontal stretch / compressionf(bx): widths divided by b; |b| > 1 squeezes.
    Reflection−f(x) flips in the x-axis; f(−x) flips in the y-axis.
    Image of a pointWhere a point of the parent lands after the transformation.
    CommuteTwo moves commute if doing them in either order gives the same result.
    Even functionf(−x) = f(x): symmetric in the y-axis.
    Odd functionf(−x) = −f(x): unchanged by a half-turn about the origin.
