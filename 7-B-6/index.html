Chapter 7 · Linear Algebra

The Determinant

One number tells you how much a move scales area, whether it flips the plane over, and whether it squashes everything flat.

Basics you are here Undergraduate Masters

On this page

  • See the determinant as the area scale factor of a move: every area gets multiplied by the same number.
  • Compute it as det = ad − bc, and see a picture of why that formula works.
  • Read its sign: negative means the move flips the plane (changes its orientation); zero means it collapses the plane flat.
  • Use the product rule: chaining two moves multiplies their determinants, det(BA) = det(B) · det(A).

1One number for every area

In 7.B.4 you watched a matrix move the 1-by-1 square built on î and ĵ into a parallelogram built on where they land. Every other square of the grid becomes an identical copy of that parallelogram. So if the parallelogram has area 5, every square of area 1 becomes area 5, every shape made of squares grows 5 times, and so does any shape at all (a circle can be filled with tinier and tinier squares).

That one number, the factor by which the move multiplies every area, is the determinant of the matrix, written det A. It gets a sign, too: positive when the move keeps the plane’s orientation (the turn from î to ĵ stays counterclockwise), negative when the move flips the plane over like a mirror. The parallelogram’s area with this sign attached is its signed area.

2Why the formula is ad − bc

For the matrix with top row a, b and bottom row c, d, î lands at [a, c] and ĵ at [b, d]. Draw the parallelogram on those two arrows and put it in a box. The box is easy: (a + b) wide and (c + d) tall. Around the parallelogram are six leftover pieces, which come in matching pairs. Take them away and watch what’s left of the arithmetic:

In symbols: (a + b)(c + d) − ac − bd − 2bc = ac + ad + bc + bd − ac − bd − 2bc = ad − bc. When ĵ ends up clockwise from î, the same arithmetic comes out negative: that’s the flip.

3Negative means flipped

Hold your left hand palm-down: the turn from your thumb to your index finger goes one way. A mirror image of that hand turns the other way, and no amount of turning will make the two match. Moves of the plane are the same. Check the small curved arrow at the origin of the After panel: it turns from î to ĵ. When it turns counterclockwise (like the original), the move kept the orientation and det > 0. When it turns clockwise, the plane was flipped, the F reads backwards, and det < 0.

Press “Swing ĵ” in the area meter. As ĵ sweeps around, the determinant rises and falls like a sine wave. It can only change sign by passing through 0, at the instants when ĵ lies on î’s line. A plane can’t be flipped smoothly without being squashed flat for an instant on the way.

4Zero means flattened

det = 0 exactly when î and ĵ land on the same line (or at the origin). Then the parallelogram has no area, and the whole plane is squashed onto a line or a point. Try the “Collapse” preset: the columns [2, 2] and [1, 1] point the same way, and ad − bc = 2·1 − 1·2 = 0.

A collapse can’t be undone. Many different points land on the same spot (in that preset, everything along each line of slope −2 lands on one point), so there’s no way to know where a landed point came from. That’s why a matrix with det = 0, called singular, has no inverse (7.B.7), and why systems of equations with det = 0 have either no solution or infinitely many (7.B.8).

5Chained moves multiply

If A multiplies areas by 2 and then B multiplies them by 3, together they multiply areas by 6. Two flips make no flip, just as (−1)·(−1) = 1. That’s the product rule: det(BA) = det(B) · det(A). It explains a lot at once: det(I) = 1, det(An) = (det A)n, and once any step in a chain flattens the plane, the whole chain has det = 0.

In 3D (7.B.10) the determinant of a 3×3 matrix plays the same role for volume: the 1×1×1 cube becomes a slanted box, and its signed volume is the determinant.

6Resizing pictures and printing designs

When you resize a photo, the screen area it covers changes by the determinant of the resize. Zoom by 2 in both directions and the matrix is 2I, with det = 2·2 = 4: the photo covers four times the screen, not twice. Stretch it ×2 sideways only and it covers twice the area, with every pixel turned into a wide rectangle.

7Now, in symbols

\[ \det\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} = ad - bc \]
Top-left times bottom-right, minus top-right times bottom-left.
\[ \text{area after the move} = |\det A| \times \text{area before} \]
The sign says whether the move flips the plane: det > 0 keeps orientation, det < 0 flips it, det = 0 flattens it.
\[ \det(BA) = \det B \cdot \det A, \qquad \det I = 1, \qquad \det(A^n) = (\det A)^n \]
The product rule: area factors multiply when moves are chained.
\[ \det A = 0 \iff \text{the columns of } A \text{ lie on one line} \iff A \text{ cannot be undone} \]
A singular matrix squashes the plane, so it has no inverse (7.B.7).

By hand, a 2×2 determinant takes seconds; bigger ones get long fast (a 3×3 has six products, a 10×10 has over three million). A computer finds them with row reduction (7.B.9):

import numpy as np                                    # NumPy: arrays of numbers
A = np.array([[2, 1], [1, 3]])                        # a move of the plane
print(round(np.linalg.det(A), 6))                     # 5.0: every area grows 5 times
B = np.array([[1, 2, 0], [0, 1, 3], [2, 0, 1]])       # a move of 3D space
print(round(np.linalg.det(B), 6))                     # 13.0: every volume grows 13 times
C = np.array([[0, 1, 0], [1, 0, 0], [0, 0, 1]])       # swap x and y: a mirror in 3D
print(round(np.linalg.det(C @ B), 6))                 # -13.0 = det(C) * det(B) = (-1) * 13

8Where this goes

  • Next (7.B.7): undoing a move. A matrix can be undone exactly when det ≠ 0, and the undo move has det = 1 / det A.
  • Later in this book: deciding whether a system of equations has exactly one solution (7.B.8), volumes in 3D (7.B.10), finding eigenvalues by solving det(A − λI) = 0 (Undergraduate tier), and the change-of-variables rule for areas in calculus, where a determinant tells how a curved map stretches area.
  • In the world: graphics engines use the sign to tell which side of a triangle faces the camera, mapmakers measure how much a map projection distorts areas, and physics uses determinants to find volumes and the direction of rotations.

9Words to know

Determinantdet A = ad − bc: the signed factor by which the move multiplies every area.
Area scale factor|det A|: how many times bigger (or smaller) every area becomes.
ParallelogramA four-sided shape with opposite sides parallel. The 1×1 square always lands on one.
Signed areaArea with a sign: positive if the turn from î to ĵ is counterclockwise, negative if clockwise.
OrientationWhich way the turn from î to ĵ goes. A move with det > 0 keeps it; det < 0 reverses it.
FlipA move that reverses orientation, like a mirror. Its determinant is negative.
Collapse (singular matrix)det = 0: the plane is squashed onto a line or a point, and the move can’t be undone.
Product ruledet(BA) = det(B) · det(A): chained moves multiply their area factors.
