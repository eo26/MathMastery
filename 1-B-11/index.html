Percentages

A percent is a fraction with a fixed bottom: out of 100. That one idea answers every percent question, as long as you keep track of what the 100% is.

1Out of a hundred

Percent means “per hundred”: from the Latin per centum. 35% is 35 out of every 100, the same as the fraction 35/100 or the decimal 0.35. Draw a 10 × 10 grid and 35% is 35 of the 100 squares. Percents are popular because everything is out of the same 100, so they’re easy to compare: 42 right out of 50 and 18 out of 24 are hard to rank, but 84% and 75% aren’t.

Every percent statement has three numbers. The whole is the full amount, the thing that counts as 100%. The part is some piece of it. The percent says how big the part is, out of 100:

\[ \text{part} = \frac{\text{percent}}{100} \times \text{whole}. \]

Know any two and you can find the third. It’s the proportion idea from 1.B.4–1.B.8 again: part is to whole as percent is to 100. The double number line below shows it: the whole lines up with 100%, and everything in between scales.

2Three questions, one picture

  • Find the part. A 4,000 mAh battery shows 35%. How much charge is left? Turn the percent into a decimal and multiply: 0.35 × 4,000 = 1,400 mAh.
  • Find the percent. 42 right out of 50. Divide part by whole, then multiply by 100: 42 ÷ 50 = 0.84 = 84%.
  • Find the whole. A download has reached 240 MB, which is 60%. If 60% is 240, then 1% is 4 and 100% is 400 MB. In one step: 240 ÷ 0.60 = 400.

To switch forms, remember that % means ÷ 100. 35% = 35/100 = 0.35 (move the decimal point two places left); 0.072 = 7.2%; 3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%. Benchmarks help: 50% is a half, 25% a quarter, 10% a tenth, 1% a hundredth. To find 10% of anything, divide by 10; 5% is half of that.

Nothing stops a percent going over 100. If your daily step goal is 10,000 and you walk 15,000, you reached 150% of the goal: one whole grid and half of another. 100% is just “the same as the whole”, not a ceiling.

3Percent change: one multiplication

A 40 cm plant grows 25%. The growth is 25% of 40 = 10 cm, so it’s now 50 cm. Faster: the new height is the old 100% plus 25%, which is 125% of the old height, so multiply by 1.25. A percent increase of r% multiplies by 1 + r/100; a percent decrease of r% multiplies by 1 − r/100 (a 60% slowdown keeps 40%: × 0.4). That number is the multiplier.

Going the other way, the percent change from an old value to a new one is

\[ \text{percent change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100\%. \]

Always divide by the old value: it’s the 100%, the thing being compared against. To find the original value before a change, divide by the multiplier. A sweater shrank 20% in the wash and is now 64 cm: it was 64 ÷ 0.8 = 80 cm.

4Changes multiply, they don’t add

A plant grows 50% one month and then a snail eats 50% of it. Back to the start? No: 40 → 60 → 30. The 50% loss was 50% of the bigger plant. In multipliers, 1.5 × 0.5 = 0.75, a 25% loss overall. For chained changes, multiply the multipliers; never add the percents.

That’s also why undoing a change takes a different percent. Shrinking 20% multiplies by 0.8; to get back you need to multiply by 1/0.8 = 1.25, a 25% increase. Every percent is a percent of something, and after a change the something is different.

5Percentage points

When the values are themselves percents, “up 10%” becomes ambiguous. A free-throw rate going from 40% to 50% went up by 10 percentage points: a plain subtraction, 50 − 40. As a percent change, it went up 25%, because 10 is 25% of the old 40. A phone battery dropping from 80% to 60% lost 20 points of charge, which is 25% of the charge it had. News stories mix these up all the time; the honest way is to say which you mean.

6Many small changes

Small repeated changes pile up in surprising ways. A short simulation shows a plant that grows 1% a day, one that shrinks 1% a day, and one that alternates:

up, down, wobble = 100.0, 100.0, 100.0      # three plants, each 100 mm tall to start
for day in range(1, 366):                   # one year, a day at a time
    up = up * 1.01                          # +1%: multiply by 1.01
    down = down * 0.99                      # −1%: multiply by 0.99
    if day % 2 == 1:                        # odd days...
        wobble = wobble * 1.01              # ...grow 1%
    else:                                   # even days...
        wobble = wobble * 0.99              # ...shrink 1%
print(round(up, 1))                         # 3778.3: about 38 times as tall
print(round(down, 1))                       # 2.6: almost nothing left
print(round(wobble, 1))                     # 99.2: +1% then −1% loses a little every time

Adding the percents would say +365%, −365% (impossible: you can’t lose more than 100%) and 0%. Multiplying gives the truth: 1.01 × 0.99 = 0.9999, so every up-and-down pair loses 0.01%.

7Traps

Compare

Easy to get wrong

✕ +50% then −50% = no change

The 50% loss is taken from 150, not 100. 100 × 1.5 × 0.5 = 75: a 25% loss overall.

✕ 40% → 50% is “up 10%”

It’s up 10 percentage points. Measured against the old 40%, that’s a 25% increase. Say which one you mean.

✕ Undo −20% with +20%

80 shrinks 20% to 64. Adding 20% of 64 gives only 76.8. To undo, divide by 0.8: 64 ÷ 0.8 = 80, which is +25%.

8Now, in symbols

With part P, whole W and percent p:

\[ p\% = \frac{p}{100}, \qquad P = \frac{p}{100}\,W, \qquad p = \frac{P}{W}\times 100, \qquad W = \frac{P}{p/100}. \]

A change of r% (negative for a decrease) multiplies by m = 1 + r/100. Chained changes multiply, and dividing undoes:

\[ \text{new} = \text{old}\times\Big(1+\frac{r}{100}\Big), \qquad \frac{\text{new}-\text{old}}{\text{old}}\times 100 = r, \qquad m_{\text{total}} = m_1 m_2 \cdots m_n, \qquad \text{old} = \frac{\text{new}}{m}. \]

Two percents a% and b% differ by b − a percentage points, a relative change of (b − a)/a × 100%.

10Words to know

Percent (%)Per hundred: 35% = 35/100 = 0.35.
WholeThe full amount, the thing that counts as 100%. Also called the base.
PartA piece of the whole, described by its percent.
Double number lineTwo scales side by side, units and percents, with the whole lined up at 100%.
Percent increase / decreaseA change described as a percent of the old value.
Percent change(new − old) ÷ old × 100%: positive for an increase, negative for a decrease.
MultiplierThe number a change multiplies by: +25% is × 1.25, −40% is × 0.6.
Original valueThe value before a change: new ÷ multiplier.
Chained changesSeveral changes one after another. Their multipliers multiply.
Percentage pointThe plain difference between two percents: 40% to 50% is 10 points.
Over 100%More than the whole: 150% of a goal is one and a half times the goal.
