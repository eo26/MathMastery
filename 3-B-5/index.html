Lines: Slope and Linear Functions

A straight line changes at a steady rate: every step to the right climbs (or drops) by the same amount. That one number, the slope, plus a starting height tells you everything about the line.

Basics you are here Undergraduate Masters

On this page

  • Find the slope of a line as rise over run, and read it as a rate of change: positive, negative, zero or undefined.
  • Read and write a linear function in slope-intercept form y = mx + b, where b is the y-intercept.
  • Use point-slope form and standard form, and convert between all three.
  • Recognise parallel lines (equal slopes) and perpendicular lines (slopes that multiply to −1).

1Steady change makes a straight line

A taxi charges $3 to get in and $2 per mile. After 0 miles you owe $3, after 1 mile $5, after 2 miles $7: every extra mile adds the same $2. Plot those pairs and they fall on a straight line. That’s the signature of a linear function: the output changes by the same amount for every equal step of the input.

The amount it changes per step is the slope. On a graph, pick any two points on the line. The horizontal distance between them is the run, the vertical distance is the rise, and the slope is rise ÷ run. For the taxi: from 1 mile to 3 miles is a run of 2, and the fare goes from $5 to $9, a rise of 4: slope 4 ÷ 2 = 2 dollars per mile. The slope is a rate of change, and it comes with units: dollars per mile, litres per minute, metres per second.

The sign tells the direction. Positive slope: the line climbs as you move right. Negative: it falls. Zero: it’s flat (horizontal), because the rise is 0. A vertical line has a run of 0, and dividing by zero is impossible, so its slope is undefined. (It isn’t a function either: one input, endless outputs. See 3.B.4.)

2Slope-intercept form: y = mx + b

Every non-vertical line can be written y = mx + b. The slope m is how much y changes per step of x. The number b is the value of y when x = 0: the height where the line crosses the y-axis, called the y-intercept. In the taxi example, y = 2x + 3: $2 per mile, $3 to start. In a story, m is the rate and b is the starting amount.

To read m and b from a graph: find where the line crosses the y-axis (that’s b), then from that point walk 1 step right and see how far the line rose or fell (that’s m). If the slope is a fraction like 2/3, it’s easier to walk 3 right and count 2 up.

3Three ways to write a line

Point-slope form y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) is the fastest way to write a line when you know one point (x₁, y₁) and the slope. It says it directly: the rise from your point equals the slope times the run from your point. Through two points? Find the slope first, then use either point.

Standard form Ax + By = C, with whole numbers, puts x and y on equal footing. It’s the natural form for “mix” problems (3 adult tickets and 2 child tickets cost $40: 3x + 2y = 40) and the only one of the three that can write a vertical line (x = 4 is 1x + 0y = 4). Its intercepts are easy: set y = 0 to get the x-intercept C/A, and x = 0 to get the y-intercept C/B.

All three describe the same line; converting is just the equation-solving moves from 3.B.2.

4Parallel and perpendicular

Two lines with the same slope rise at the same rate, so they never meet: they are parallel. (Unless they also have the same intercept, in which case they are the same line.)

Perpendicular lines meet at a right angle. Turn a slope staircase a quarter turn: a run of 1 with a rise of m becomes a run of m with a fall of 1 (or a rise of 1 going left). So the new slope is −1/m: flip the fraction and change the sign. Equivalently, the two slopes multiply to −1. A slope of 2/3 is perpendicular to −3/2. The exception: horizontal and vertical lines are perpendicular, but the vertical one has no slope to multiply.

5Slope in the world

Builders and road crews talk about slope all the time, in different clothes. A road grade of 6% means 6 m of climb per 100 m across: slope 0.06. A roof pitch of “6 in 12” means 6 inches up per 12 inches across: slope 1/2. A wheelchair ramp may rise at most 1 inch per 12 inches: slope 1/12. They are all rise over run.

Comparing two plans with different starting fees and rates means comparing two lines: the one with the lower start wins at first, the one with the lower rate wins in the long run, and they cost the same where the lines cross (a system of equations, 3.B.6).

6Traps and non-examples

Compare

Easy to get wrong

✕ Run over rise

From (0, 1) to (4, 3): rise 2, run 4, slope 2/4 = 1/2. Writing 4/2 = 2 gives a much steeper line. Slope is rise (up) over run (across).

✕ Mixed-up subtraction order

(y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁) and (y₁ − y₂)/(x₁ − x₂) both work. Mixing them, (y₂ − y₁)/(x₁ − x₂), flips the sign: an uphill line comes out downhill.

✕ Not every straight-ish rule is linear

A tank that fills 1, 2, 4, 8 litres in successive minutes doesn’t change by equal amounts per step, so its graph curves. Equal steps in x must give equal changes in y.

7Now, in symbols

\[ m = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} \quad (x_1 \ne x_2) \]
Δ (delta) means “change in”. The slope is the same for any two points on the line.
\[ y = mx + b \qquad y - y_1 = m(x - x_1) \qquad Ax + By = C \]
Slope-intercept, point-slope and standard form. From standard form (B ≠ 0): m = −A/B, b = C/B.
\[ f(x) = mx + b: \qquad f(x + 1) - f(x) = m \]
The defining property of a linear function: each step of 1 in the input adds m to the output.
\[ \text{parallel: } m_1 = m_2, \qquad \text{perpendicular: } m_1 m_2 = -1 \;\iff\; m_2 = -\frac{1}{m_1} \]

Fitting a line to measured data, where points don’t line up perfectly, is a job for a computer. Here it recovers the taxi’s rate and starting fee from a few rides:

import numpy as np                         # numerical arrays

miles = np.array([1, 2.5, 4, 7])            # distances of four rides
fares = np.array([5, 8, 11, 17])            # what each ride cost
m, b = np.polyfit(miles, fares, 1)          # best straight line, degree 1: returns slope, intercept
print(round(m, 2), round(b, 2))             # 2.0 3.0: $2 per mile, $3 to start
print(m * 10 + b)                           # predicted fare for 10 miles: 23.0

9Words to know

SlopeHow steep a line is: rise ÷ run, the change in y per unit change in x. Usually written m.
Rise / runThe vertical change and the horizontal change between two points.
Rate of changeA slope with units: dollars per mile, litres per minute.
Linear functionA function whose output changes by the same amount for every equal step of input; its graph is a straight line.
y-interceptWhere the line crosses the y-axis; the value b = y when x = 0.
x-interceptWhere the line crosses the x-axis; the input that gives output 0 (a zero).
Slope-intercept formy = mx + b.
Point-slope formy − y₁ = m(x − x₁): a line through (x₁, y₁) with slope m.
Standard formAx + By = C, usually with whole numbers. Can write vertical lines.
Zero slopeA horizontal line, y = b: no rise.
Undefined slopeA vertical line, x = c: no run, so no slope.
Parallel linesLines with equal slopes that never meet.
Perpendicular linesLines meeting at a right angle; their slopes multiply to −1.
Grade / pitchSlope as builders write it: a percent (road grade) or “rise in 12” (roof pitch).
