Laws of Sines and Cosines

SOH-CAH-TOA needs a right angle. Two laws handle every other triangle, so three measurements are enough to find everything else: across a lake, between two ships, or out to a distant fire.

Basics you are here Undergraduate Masters

On this page

  • Use the law of sines, a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C, and see why it works by dropping a height.
  • Use the law of cosines, c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C: Pythagoras plus a correction for angles that aren’t 90°.
  • Decide which law fits what you know (SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, SSA), and spot the ambiguous case, where two different triangles fit the same facts.
  • Apply them to navigation, triangulation, and the area of a triangle, ½ab sin C.

1Any triangle, labeled the same way

Name the corners A, B, C, and use the same letter, lowercase, for the side across from each corner: side a is opposite corner A, and so on. (We also use A for the angle at corner A.) The angles always add to 180°, and the longest side is always across from the biggest angle.

2Why the law of sines works

Drop the height h from C straight down to the line through A and B. It splits the triangle into two right triangles, and each one gives h with SOH-CAH-TOA (5.B.2):

in the left one: h = b sin A    in the right one: h = a sin B

Both equal h, so b sin A = a sin B. Divide both sides by sin A sin B and you get a/sin A = b/sin B. Dropping the height from a different corner brings in c/sin C the same way. In words: each side is proportional to the sine of the angle across from it.

If an angle is obtuse, the height lands outside the triangle (try the “Obtuse at A” shape). The argument still works, because sin(180° − θ) = sin θ (5.B.2): the outside angle and the inside angle have the same sine.

3The law of cosines: Pythagoras, corrected

When C is a right angle, c² = a² + b². Close the angle and the far ends of a and b come together, so c gets shorter. Open it past 90° and c gets longer. The law of cosines says exactly how much:

c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C

The correction −2ab cos C is negative for angles under 90° (cos is positive), zero at 90° (Pythagoras), and positive for angles over 90°. Like the law of sines, it comes from dropping a height and using Pythagoras on the two right triangles. Watch the correction change as the hinge opens:

4Which law, when?

Three facts about a triangle (with at least one side) usually pin it down. Shorthand: S for a side you know, A for an angle you know, in the order they come around the triangle.

You knowExampleUse
SSS: all three sidesa, b, cCosines, solved for an angle: cos C = (a² + b² − c²) / (2ab)
SAS: two sides and the angle between thema, C, bCosines for the third side, then either law for the rest
ASA or AAS: two angles and a sideA, B, aThird angle = 180° − the other two, then sines
SSA: two sides and an angle not between thema, b, ASines, but watch out: there may be two triangles, one, or none (next section)

The rule of thumb: the law of sines needs a matched pair, a side together with the angle across from it. If you don’t have a matched pair, start with the law of cosines.

5The ambiguous case

Know angle A, the side b next to it, and the side a across from it, and try to build the triangle: side a hangs from corner C like a swinging gate, and corner B must land where the gate meets the ground. Depending on a’s length, it may miss the ground, touch it once, or cross it twice. Two crossings mean two different triangles fit the same three facts, so the law of sines gives two possible angles B (an angle and its partner, 180° minus it, have the same sine).

6Navigation, triangulation and area

Two legs of a trip. A boat sails 8 km east, turns 50° to the left and sails 5 km more. How far is it from where it started? The turn makes an inside angle of 180° − 50° = 130° at the corner, which is SAS: a job for the law of cosines.

Triangulation. Two fire lookouts stand a known distance apart. Each one measures the angle between the line to the other tower and the line to the smoke. That’s ASA, so the law of sines finds how far the fire is from each tower, without anyone going near it. Surveyors mapped whole countries this way, one triangle at a time.

Area. Half base times height works for any triangle, and the height is b sin C if the base is a. So the area of a triangle from two sides and the angle between them is ½ab sin C, with no height to measure. It’s how a surveyor finds the area of a triangular plot of land from its two fences and the corner angle.

7Now, in symbols

\[ \frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C} \]
The law of sines: each side over the sine of the angle across from it.
\[ c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C, \qquad \cos C = \frac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab} \]
The law of cosines, and the same law solved for an angle (SSS). Swap letters for the other sides and angles.
\[ A + B + C = 180^\circ, \qquad \text{Area} = \tfrac12\,ab\sin C \]
The angle sum, and the area from two sides and the angle between them.

Solving a triangle from its three sides takes a few lines of code (angles in degrees at the end):

import math                                              # Python's built-in math tools
a, b, c = 7, 5, 9                                        # the three sides (SSS)
C = math.degrees(math.acos((a*a + b*b - c*c) / (2*a*b))) # law of cosines solved for angle C
A = math.degrees(math.asin(a * math.sin(math.radians(C)) / c))   # law of sines: a / sin A = c / sin C (C is the largest angle, so A is acute)
B = 180 - A - C                                          # the angles add to 180°
area = 0.5 * a * b * math.sin(math.radians(C))           # area = ½ ab sin C
print(round(A, 1), round(B, 1), round(C, 1), round(area, 2))   # 50.7 33.6 95.7 17.41

8Where this goes

  • Next (5.B.4): waves and periodic functions, where sine and cosine describe things that repeat.
  • Later in this book: the law of cosines in vector form is the dot product (7.B.3): |u − v|² = |u|² + |v|² − 2 u·v. Triangle areas become the determinant (7.B.6): ½|ad − bc|. And on a sphere, the same ideas become spherical trigonometry for routes across the Earth.
  • In the world: surveying and map-making, ship and aircraft navigation, locating a phone or a lightning strike from several receivers, robot arms working out joint angles, and astronomy measuring distances to nearby stars from two points of Earth’s orbit.

Height (altitude)The perpendicular from a corner to the line through the opposite side. It can land outside the triangle.
