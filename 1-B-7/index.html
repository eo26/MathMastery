Converting Customary and Metric Units

An inch and a centimeter measure the same world with different-sized steps. Converting between them is a proportional relationship, and the constant of proportionality is the conversion factor.

Basics you are here Undergraduate Masters

On this page

  • See unit conversion as a proportional relationship: metric = k × customary.
  • Use the conversion factor k (the constant of proportionality) in both directions: multiply one way, divide the other.
  • Know exact factors and friendly ones for mental math: 1 in = 2.54 cm, 1 mi ≈ 1.6 km, 1 kg ≈ 2.2 lb.
  • Explain why temperature conversion is not proportional.

1Two rulers, one world

The United States mostly measures with customary units: inches, feet, miles, ounces and pounds. Almost everywhere else, and in every science lab, people use metric units: centimeters, meters, kilometers, grams and kilograms. A pencil doesn’t change length when you pick up a different ruler, but the number you read does.

Lay an inch ruler and a centimeter ruler side by side, both starting at 0. Wherever you look, the centimeter reading is about 2½ times the inch reading. Exactly, it’s 2.54 times: 1 inch = 2.54 centimeters, by definition. And 0 inches is 0 centimeters, and doubling the inches doubles the centimeters. This is a proportional relationship, and 2.54 is its constant of proportionality. When it converts units, people call k the conversion factor.

2Multiply or divide?

The machine runs both ways. Going from customary to metric, multiply by k. Going back from metric to customary, run the machine backwards: divide by k.

A sanity check that never fails: smaller units mean bigger numbers. A centimeter is smaller than an inch, so a length in centimeters is always a bigger number than the same length in inches. If you convert 10 inches and get 3.9 centimeters, you went the wrong way. A kilogram is heavier than a pound, so a weight in kilograms is a smaller number than the same weight in pounds.

3Exact factors and friendly ones

Some factors are exact by definition (1 inch is exactly 2.54 cm). Others have long decimals. For quick estimates in your head, round them to friendly numbers. The ≈ sign means “about equal to.”

4The one that isn’t proportional: temperature

Not every conversion is a simple multiply. Water freezes at 0 °C, but that’s 32 °F. Zero doesn’t go with zero, so Celsius and Fahrenheit are not proportional. Doubling doesn’t work either: 10 °C is 50 °F, but 20 °C is 68 °F, not 100 °F.

The rule is °F = 1.8 × °C + 32. That “+ 32” is a starting amount, exactly like the taxi’s $3 fee in 1.B.5 and 1.B.6. The graph is a straight line, but it misses the origin.

    5Now, in symbols

    In the explorer the customary amount was x, the metric amount was y, and the number in the machine was the conversion factor k. Converting is the same proportional rule from 1.B.5, run in either direction:

    \[ y = k \cdot x \qquad\qquad x = \frac{y}{k} \]
    For inches and centimeters: \(\text{cm} = 2.54 \times \text{in}\) and \(\text{in} = \text{cm} \div 2.54\).

    Because the relationship is proportional, any two measurements of the rulers form a proportion:

    \[ \frac{2.54\ \text{cm}}{1\ \text{in}} = \frac{30.48\ \text{cm}}{12\ \text{in}} \]
    Both equal \(k = 2.54\) centimeters per inch. Temperature can’t do this: \(\tfrac{50}{10} \ne \tfrac{68}{20}\).

    7Words to know

    Proportional relationshipTwo amounts where y = k · x. Length and weight conversions are proportional; temperature is not.
    Constant of proportionalityThe number k. In a unit conversion it’s the conversion factor: 2.54 cm per inch.
    ProportionA statement that two ratios are equal: 2.54/1 = 30.48/12.
    Conversion factorThe number you multiply by to change units: how many of the new unit make 1 of the old.
    Customary unitsThe US system: inches, feet, miles, ounces, pounds.
    Metric unitsThe system used by most of the world and in science: centimeters, meters, kilometers, grams, kilograms.
    ≈ (approximately equal)“About equal to.” 1 mile ≈ 1.6 km.

    8Check yourself
