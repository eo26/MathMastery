Graphing Proportional Relationships

Turn every row of a ratio table into a point, and a proportional relationship draws itself: a straight line that starts at zero and climbs k for every step of 1.

Basics you are here Undergraduate Masters

On this page

  • Plot a ratio table as points on a graph, and say what any point means.
  • Recognize a proportional relationship on a graph: a straight line through the origin (0, 0).
  • Read the constant of proportionality k from the graph: the point (1, k), or “over 1, up k”.
  • Compare relationships by steepness: the steeper line has the bigger k.

1Every row is a point

A graph is a picture of a table. Draw two number lines that meet at 0: one going across for the first amount x, and one going up for the second amount y. The flat page between them is called the coordinate plane, and the corner where both are 0 is the origin.

Now take a row of the lemonade table: 4 scoops with 6 cups. Start at the origin, go over 4, then up 6, and put a dot. That dot is the ordered pair (4, 6). The order matters: x first, then y. Every point on the graph is a sentence: “4 scoops go with 6 cups.”

2Three things every proportional graph does

  1. The points line up in a straight line. Each step of 1 to the right adds exactly k going up, every time. The little staircase under the line shows it: every step is the same size, so the line never bends.
  2. The line goes through the origin (0, 0). Zero scoops go with zero cups; zero hours of driving go with zero miles. That’s the “check zero” test from 1.B.5, drawn as a picture.
  3. The point above x = 1 is (1, k). Go over 1, and the line is exactly k high. That’s the same “find the one” idea from 1.B.5, and it’s why k is how steep the line is: over 1, up k.

Because the line is straight and passes through the origin, any point on it gives you k: divide its y by its x. The point (4, 6) gives 6 ÷ 4 = 1½, exactly what (1, 1½) says.

4What “not proportional” looks like

Remember the taxi from 1.B.5: $3 to get in, then $2 per mile. Its graph is a straight line too, but look where it starts. At 0 miles you already owe $3, so the line begins at (0, 3), above the origin. A straight line that misses the origin is not proportional.     5Now, in symbols

    On the graph, the across direction was labeled x, the up direction y, and the first step of the staircase was “over 1, up k.” Every point on the line obeys the same rule from 1.B.5:

    \[ y = k \cdot x \]
    The graph of this equation is always a straight line through \((0,0)\) and \((1,k)\).

    Pick any point \((x, y)\) on the line except the origin, and it hands you \(k\):

    \[ k = \frac{y}{x} = \frac{\text{up}}{\text{over}} \]
    “How far up for each 1 over.” For the point \((4, 6)\): \(k = \tfrac{6}{4} = 1\tfrac{1}{2}\).

    Two points on the same line also make a proportion: \(\tfrac{6}{4} = \tfrac{9}{6}\), because both equal \(k\). The taxi’s rule is \(y = 2x + 3\). That extra \(+\,3\) is what lifts its line off the origin and breaks the proportion.

    6Where this goes

    • Next (1.B.7): converting between units, like inches and centimeters, is a proportional relationship too. Its graph is a straight line through the origin, and k is the conversion factor.
    • Later in this book: the steepness k becomes the slope of a line in Algebra, and the taxi’s rule \(y = 2x + 3\) is the general straight line \(y = mx + b\). In Calculus, the idea of “how steep is it right here?” works even for curves that bend: that’s the derivative.
    • In the world: distance–time graphs on a fitness app, how a bathtub fills at a steady rate, and the cost of buying more of something at a fixed price are all straight lines through the origin. When the line is steeper, the rate is faster.

    7Words to know

    Proportional relationshipTwo amounts where y = k · x. On a graph: a straight line through the origin.
    Constant of proportionalityThe number k. On the graph it’s the height of the line at x = 1, and how steep the line is: over 1, up k.
    ProportionA statement that two ratios are equal. Any two points on a proportional line make one: 6/4 = 9/6.
    Coordinate planeThe flat grid made by an across number line (x) and an up number line (y).
    Ordered pairA point written (x, y): go over x, then up y. Order matters.
    OriginThe point (0, 0), where the two number lines cross.
    SteepnessHow fast a line climbs as you move right. For a proportional line, it’s k.
