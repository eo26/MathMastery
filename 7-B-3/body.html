Chapter 7 · Linear Algebra

The Dot Product

Multiply two lists entry by entry and add up: you get one number. That number secretly measures how much two arrows point the same way, and it powers everything from shopping totals to solar panels to recommendation engines.

Basics you are here Undergraduate Masters

On this page

  • Compute the dot product u · v = u₁v₁ + u₂v₂ (+ … for longer lists).
  • See it as a shadow: the length of u times how far v’s projection reaches along u.
  • Read the sign: positive means roughly the same way, zero means perpendicular, negative means roughly opposite.
  • Use it to find the angle between vectors, a vector’s length (v · v = ‖v‖²), the work done by a push, and how similar two lists are.

1A product that gives one number

At the store you buy 4 apples at $2, 6 eggs at $1 and 1 loaf of bread at $3. Your total is a dot product of two lists: multiply matching entries, then add.

appleseggsbread
price ($)213
quantity461
price × quantity863total 17

[2, 1, 3] · [4, 6, 1] = 2·4 + 1·6 + 3·1 = 17. Notice the answer is not a list but a single number: a scalar. That’s why it’s also called the scalar product. For two arrows in the plane it’s the same recipe: u · v = u₁v₁ + u₂v₂.

The surprise is what that number means when the lists are arrows. It measures how much the two arrows point the same way.

2The dot product is a shadow

Shine a light straight down onto the line through u. The arrow v casts a shadow on that line: its projection. The dot product is

u · v = (length of u) × (signed length of v’s shadow on u)

If the shadow falls forward along u, the dot product is positive. If v stands straight up from u (they’re perpendicular), there’s no shadow at all and the dot product is 0. If the shadow falls backward, it’s negative. Drag the tips below, and press “Spin v” to watch the dot product rise and fall as v turns: the graph traces a cosine wave, just like the waves in 5.B.4.

3Finding the angle, and the length

The shadow of v has length ‖v‖ cos θ, where θ is the angle between the vectors (the cosine from 5.B.1: adjacent side over hypotenuse). So the shadow picture says

u · v = ‖u‖ ‖v‖ cos θ   ⟹   cos θ = u · v / (‖u‖ ‖v‖)

That turns the angle between two arrows into arithmetic. Example: u = [1, 0], v = [1, 1]. Then u · v = 1, ‖u‖ = 1, ‖v‖ = √2, so cos θ = 1/√2 ≈ 0.707 and θ = 45°. The sign of the dot product follows the sign of cos θ: positive for angles under 90°, zero at exactly 90°, negative beyond.

Dot a vector with itself and the angle is 0, so cos θ = 1 and v · v = ‖v‖². That’s the Pythagorean length from 7.B.1 in disguise: [3, 4] · [3, 4] = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5². (Try the “v · v” preset above.)

4Only the part along the way counts

A solar panel catches the most sunlight when it faces the sun head-on. Tilt it away and the same panel intercepts a narrower beam. The fraction it catches is cos θ, where θ is the angle between the sun’s direction and the panel’s normal (the arrow sticking straight out of its face): a dot product of two unit arrows.

Physics uses the same idea for work, the energy a push puts into moving something. Push a cart with a force F while it rolls a distance d along the floor: the work is W = F · d. Only the part of your push along the floor moves the cart; the part pressing down into the floor does nothing useful.

5How alike are two lists?

A streaming service asks people to rate movies from −2 (hated it) to +2 (loved it). Each person’s ratings form a vector. Where two people agree, the matching entries have the same sign and their product is positive; where they disagree, the product is negative. So the dot product adds up agreement minus disagreement.

One catch: someone who rates everything strongly gets bigger dot products just for being loud. Dividing by both lengths fixes that, and gives the cosine of the “angle” between the two taste vectors, called cosine similarity: 1 means identical taste, 0 means unrelated, −1 means opposite. It works in 5 dimensions just as well as in 2, even though no one can draw the arrows.

6Now, in symbols

The two descriptions of the dot product, by components and by geometry:

\[ \mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = u_1 v_1 + u_2 v_2 + \cdots + u_n v_n = \lVert\mathbf{u}\rVert\,\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert\cos\theta \]
Left: multiply matching entries and add. Right: lengths times the cosine of the angle between them.
\[ \cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{\lVert\mathbf{u}\rVert\,\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert}, \qquad \mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = 0 \iff \mathbf{u}\perp\mathbf{v}, \qquad \mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v} = \lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert^2 \]
The angle formula, the perpendicular test, and the length formula.
\[ \text{shadow of } \mathbf{v} \text{ on } \mathbf{u} = \frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{\lVert\mathbf{u}\rVert}, \qquad \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{v} = \frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{u}}\,\mathbf{u} \]
The signed shadow length, and the shadow itself as a vector along u.

For long lists, a computer does the multiply-and-add. This is how similarity between millions of items is computed:

import numpy as np                              # NumPy: arrays of numbers
a = np.array([2, 1, -2, 0, 1])                  # person A's movie ratings
b = np.array([2, 2, -1, -1, 1])                 # person B's ratings
dot = a @ b                                     # dot product: multiply matching entries and add -> 9
cos = dot / (np.linalg.norm(a) * np.linalg.norm(b))   # divide by both lengths: cosine similarity
print(dot, round(cos, 3))                       # 9 0.818  (very similar taste)

7Where this goes

  • Next (7.B.4): a matrix times a vector is a stack of dot products, one per row. Each output entry is “this row · the input.”
  • Later in this book: projections become the heart of least-squares fitting (finding the best line through data, Chapter 10), perpendicular (orthogonal) vectors build convenient coordinate systems in the Undergraduate tier, and in calculus the rate of change in a direction is a dot product with the gradient.
  • In the world: totals and weighted scores, lighting and shading in every 3D video game (brightness = light direction · surface normal), work and power in physics, and search engines and AI models comparing meanings by the cosine similarity of very long vectors.

8Words to know

Dot productu · v = u₁v₁ + u₂v₂ + …: multiply matching entries and add. The result is a single number.
ScalarA single number, as opposed to a vector. The dot product of two vectors is a scalar.
Projection (shadow)The part of one vector lying along another: what’s left after dropping straight down onto the other’s line.
Angle between vectorsθ, from cos θ = u · v / (‖u‖ ‖v‖), between 0° and 180°.
Perpendicular (orthogonal)At a right angle. Two nonzero vectors are perpendicular exactly when their dot product is 0.
Normal vectorAn arrow sticking straight out of a surface, perpendicular to it.
WorkThe energy a force puts into motion: W = F · d.
Cosine similarityu · v divided by both lengths: from −1 (opposite) through 0 (unrelated) to 1 (same direction).
